# WP83 — source-closed Ingham lower bound from the formal MV inequality\n\nDate: 2026-10-04\nStatus: **SEPARATED-FREQUENCY ENERGY FLOOR CLOSED WITH EXPLICIT CONSTANT 26; CLUSTERED BLOCK EXTENSION OPEN**\n\nThe public formal repository proves the Montgomery--Vaughan bilinear Hilbert inequality with an admissible absolute constant C=26. This immediately gives a lower frame bound for exponentials on a finite interval.\n\n## 1. Exact interval identity\n\nLet lambda_1,...,lambda_M be distinct real frequencies and a_r complex coefficients. Put\n\n    S(t)=sum_r a_r exp(i lambda_r t).\n\nFor H>0, direct integration gives\n\n    integral_0^H |S(t)|^2 dt\n      = H sum_r |a_r|^2 + Off,\n\nwhere\n\n    Off\n      = sum_{r!=s}\n          a_r conj(a_s)\n          [exp(i(lambda_r-lambda_s)H)-1]\n          / [i(lambda_r-lambda_s)].\n\n## 2. Two MV-Hilbert applications\n\nAssume a uniform separation\n\n    |lambda_r-lambda_s| >= Delta\n\nfor r!=s.\n\nThe first endpoint part of Off is the MV bilinear form applied to\n\n    x_r=a_r exp(i lambda_r H),\n    z_r=a_r exp(i lambda_r H),\n\nand the second endpoint part to x_r=z_r=a_r.\n\nUsing the formal constant C=26 and the admissible choice delta_r=Delta gives\n\n    |Off|\n      <= (2C/Delta) sum_r |a_r|^2\n      = (52/Delta) sum_r |a_r|^2.\n\nTherefore\n\n    boxed:\n    integral_0^H |S(t)|^2 dt\n      >= (H-52/Delta) sum_r |a_r|^2.\n\nIn particular, if\n\n    Delta >= 104/H,\n\nthen\n\n    boxed:\n    integral_0^H |S(t)|^2 dt\n      >= (H/2) sum_r |a_r|^2.\n\nThis is an unconditional, source-closed Ingham-type lower bound with a completely explicit separation threshold.\n\n## 3. Endpoint placement for deep zeros\n\nThe scalar energy identity uses\n\n    g(v)>=L-2w-|v|.\n\nChoose a positive interval\n\n    I_theta=[(1-2theta)L, (1-theta)L]\n\nwith fixed 0<theta<1/4 and T large enough that 2w<=theta L/2.\n\nThen\n\n    |I_theta|=theta L\n\nand throughout the interval\n\n    g(v)>=theta L/2.\n\nA depth-delta exponential loses at most the factor\n\n    exp(-2 theta delta L)=X^(-2 theta delta)\n\nbetween the upper and lower endpoints. Thus the separated-frequency floor retains the power scale\n\n    X^(2 delta(1-2theta))\n\nat Frobenius-energy level.\n\nLetting theta be an arbitrarily small fixed constant makes the exponent arbitrarily close to the optimal X^(2delta) scale, while the required frequency separation is\n\n    Delta_theta = 104/(theta L).\n\n## 4. Why clustering is unavoidable\n\nThe average zero spacing is itself of order 1/L. Hence the explicit separated threshold 104/(theta L) is on the natural microscopic scale but with a large constant.\n\nZeros closer than this must be grouped before the Ingham lower bound is used.\n\nFor a component of at most four off-line pairs, the correct grouped basis is the confluent/divided-difference basis from `WP83_TWO_WINDOW_CLUSTER_ENERGY.md`. The public formal source additionally proves spacing-sum estimates with powers two and four, which are the right tools for bounding interactions between these finite jet blocks.\n\n## 5. New SC4 subgate\n\nSC4 now decomposes into two finite tasks:\n\n1. **internal block floor:** already derived in confluent coordinates for size <=4;\n2. **block-Ingham extension:** prove that components separated by 104/(theta L) retain a lower energy floor when each component is replaced by a polynomial-exponential block of degree <=3.\n\nThe second task is finite-order. Its off-diagonal kernels are derivatives of\n\n    [exp(i Delta H)-1]/Delta,\n\nso only inverse powers through Delta^-4 occur. The source-closed `MV/Spacing.lean` estimates\n\n    sum delta_t/(freq_s-freq_t)^2 <= 9/delta_s,\n\n    sum delta_t/(freq_s-freq_t)^4 <= 27/delta_s^3,\n\nand its two-point inequality are tailored to exactly these denominators.\n\nClosing this block-Ingham step would establish the <=4 separated-component part of SC4 without any zero-spacing assumption inside a component.\n
