## 1. Evaluation vectors and exact full-grid Gram kernel

For a complex spectral point z define the full-grid evaluation vector

    u_z = ( phi_hat(z-tau_k) )_{k in Z}.

C26 Lemma 2.2, analytically continued in the spectral variables as used in its zero-side discussion, gives

    sum_k phi_hat(z-tau_k) phi_hat(w-tau_k)
      = L * Phi(z-w).

Because phi is real and even,

    <u_z,u_w> = L * Phi(z-conj(w)),

and

    ||u_z||^2 = L * Phi(z-conj(z)).

Thus every principal-angle question among zero-evaluation vectors is encoded by the explicit entire kernel Phi.

## 2. One off-line pair

For an off-line pair z,conj(z), write the real pair contribution schematically as

    H_z = 2 m Re(u_z u_z^T).

Equivalently, if u_z=a+ib with a,b real,

    H_z = 2m( a a^T - b b^T ).

It has at most two nonzero eigenvalues and is indefinite when a,b are linearly independent. Its large self-pair/Frobenius scale is the X^(2 delta) quantity noted by C26, delta=|beta-1/2|.

## 3. Exact sufficient isolation condition

Let U be the two-dimensional target evaluation space generated by u_z and u_conj(z), and let V be the span of all background evaluation vectors in the active window.

If the smallest principal angle between U and V is bounded below by sigma>0, then the orthogonal projection of U to V^perp retains at least a sigma fraction of every target vector. Restricting the full zero-side form to V^perp kills the background exactly while retaining a target hyperbolic block at scale at least sigma^2 times its uncancelled pair scale.

Equivalently, in Gram-matrix language, if

    G = [[G_UU, G_UV], [G_VU, G_VV]],

then a sufficient condition is a uniform lower bound on the Schur complement

    S_U = G_UU - G_UV G_VV^dagger G_VU

relative to G_UU:

    S_U >= sigma^2 G_UU.

This is the precise spectral isolation certificate needed by a direct Proposition-R proof.

## 4. Why critical Gabor spacing does not give a uniform sigma

The kernel formula itself rules out any spacing-only uniform lower bound.

For w->z, continuity of Phi gives

    <u_z,u_w> / (||u_z|| ||u_w||) -> 1.

Hence the Gram determinant of {u_z,u_w} tends to zero and the smallest principal angle tends to zero.

C26 explicitly states that its inertia argument is indifferent to coincidences or near-dependences among the evaluation functionals, including a pair very close to the line. The public theorem assumes no minimum zero spacing in ordinate. Therefore critical Gabor sampling does not prevent arbitrarily ill-conditioned evaluation clusters.

Consequently:

    **there is no uniform isolation constant sigma available from Gabor spacing alone.**

Any Proposition-R globalization lemma must use additional structure beyond the frame spacing: depth ordering, a cluster theorem, a special extremal vector, a positivity identity, or some other zero-specific input.

## 5. Exact cancellation criterion

For abstract pair matrices the cancellation obstruction is real. If two evaluation vectors satisfy v=i*u, then

    2 Re(v v^T) = -2 Re(u u^T),

so the two hyperbolic pair matrices cancel exactly.

The Gabor kernel does not prove that actual zero-evaluation vectors can realize this exact phase relation, but the near-collinearity result shows that a uniform anti-cancellation bound cannot follow merely from pairwise separation of the sampling grid.

## 6. What would be enough to revive Proposition R

A theorem-grade globalization step may take any of the following forms:

1. **principal-angle form:** prove the Schur-complement bound above for a deepest pair or deepest cluster;
2. **depth-gap form:** show a deepest pair dominates all shallower background contributions after localization;
3. **cluster form:** allow a near-maximal cluster but prove its aggregate restricted form has operator norm comparable to the deepest X^delta scale;
4. **duality form:** construct an explicit coefficient vector c for which the chosen pair contributes with fixed sign and every other zero is quantitatively smaller.
