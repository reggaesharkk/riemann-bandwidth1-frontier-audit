## 1. Gaussian comparison ensemble

Let g_p be independent standard real Gaussians and define

    sigma_t=sqrt(t(1-t)),

    H_t^G
      =
      H_0
      + tP
      + sigma_t
        sum_p g_p G_p.

Then the Bernoulli and Gaussian scalar coefficients have matching first two
moments:

    E eta_p=E(sigma_t g_p)=0,

    E eta_p^2
      = E(sigma_t g_p)^2
      = t(1-t).

## 2. One-coordinate resolvent expansion

Fix all coordinates except p and write

    A=H_base-zI,

    R_0=A^(-1).

For a real scalar x,

    R(x)
      =
      (A+xG_p)^(-1)

has the exact expansion

    R(x)
      =
      R_0
      - x R_0 G_p R_0
      + x^2 R_0 G_p R_0 G_p R_0

with

    ||Rem_3(x)||op

      |x|^3
      eta^(-4)
      ||G_p||^3

up to a harmless fixed factor when the final resolvent is bounded by
eta^(-1).

The normalized trace obeys the same bound.

## 3. Matching first two moments kills the first two Taylor terms

## 5. z-derivatives and bounded rational consumers
