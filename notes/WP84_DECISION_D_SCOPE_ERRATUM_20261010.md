# WP84 Decision D scope erratum — 2026-10-10

## Correction

The original Decision D note and certificate label the endpoint-score input as source-certified and classify the direct first-strike consumer as D2. That source-specific conclusion was too broad. Decision D's Markov and nonnegative-energy restriction calculation is valid **conditional on** bounded means for the two endpoint scores and an exact identification of those scores with the consumer's endpoint conditions. The cited one-prime mean-value estimate does not establish those hypotheses for a separate pair-frequency feature.

## Exact frequency-support issue

The feature examined in F1 has terms at frequencies represented by products (m=pq), with two source factors potentially as large as the natural cutoff (N=\lfloor Xe^{-\sqrt L}\rfloor). Its supported integer length can therefore be as large as (N^2). The wider balanced-product pool in the cited second-mass work permits support up to (X^2). No executable source gate (pq\le N), complete fixed-(R) feature generator, or feature-to-consumer identity was found. Bounded coefficient square mass does not shorten frequency support. On Decision B's window (U_T\sim T e^{-\sqrt L/2}), a generic mean-value bound carries (1+N_{\rm feat}/U_T); this is not (O_R(1)) when (N_{\rm feat}\) may be (N^2) (or (X^2)). F1 is therefore a proof-gap result, not a counterexample to the desired product-score estimate.

F1B derives the conditional maximal majorant (\mathcal B_T\ll_R L^4(1+N^2/U_T)) for a fixed-coefficient degree-two feature with interval/prefix truncation and bounded collapsed coefficient mass. For its specific normalized rank-two endpoint payment this gives (O_R(L^3/X+L^3e^{-3\sqrt L/2})=o(1)), even though (\mathcal B_T) itself grows. This payment conclusion requires (N^2) support and the consumer to use that payment without a separate bounded-score gate; the broader (X^2) pool does not satisfy it. The exact feature generator, its cutoff/truncation hypotheses, and its consumer identity have not been matched to the authentic Decision D source family, so F1B does not close that source application or prove the asserted (O_R(1)) pair-feature mean.

\[
S_R(k)=\sum_{|t|\le12R}\bigl(|\alpha_{k+t}|^2+|\beta_{k+t}|^2\bigr),

\[
Q(t)=\sum_{p^v\le N}-\frac{\Lambda(p^v)}{a_\phi(T)L\sqrt{p^v}}e^{it\log(p^v)}.

With all prime powers retained, coefficient square mass (O(1)), circular log-frequency gap at least (1/((N+1)L)), and (N/U_T\to0), the discrete large-sieve and cell-sampling argument gives an (O_R(1)) mean for this score on each of Decision B's exact endpoint windows. The lower- and upper-endpoint good sets each have density at least (1/2); their Cartesian product has Decision B pair density at least (1/4). Restricting Decision B's nonnegative crossing-energy average to that same set selects a common section with (E(I)=O(\log d)). The local row bound then pays the fixed-(R), (b=5,6) weighted reprojection error (4bRK_R^b/d'=o(1)).

This F1C result supports the **direct one-prime first-strike** endpoint consumer. It does not identify the product-frequency feature of Decision D with (S_R), does not prove Decision D's pair-feature mean, and does not apply to the source-masked determinant callback. It also does not prove raw cubic deletion from the original full interval or the signed fifth/sixth arithmetic gate.
